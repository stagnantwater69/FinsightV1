## How this form is organised

The manuscript defines three things this study must find out (Chapter III, Experiments 1–3). This form measures each of them directly:

| Part | What it measures | Manuscript success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Part II | Usefulness and relevance for organising records and improving financial awareness | **At least 70% positive** |
| Part III | Clarity and decision-support value of the overviews, insights, spending-impact and recovery information | **Majority positive** |
| Part IV | Usability, accuracy and ease of record management — manual entry, receipt review, categorisation, duplicate checking | **Majority positive** |

"Positive" means a response of **4 (Agree)** or **5 (Strongly Agree)**.

# Scoring guide (for the research team — remove before handing to participants)

## Computing the results

For each part, a response of **4 or 5** counts as positive.

```
Positive % for a part  =  (number of 4s and 5s across that part's statements)
                          ÷ (number of statements × number of respondents) × 100
```

Report the mean score per statement as well, so weak individual items are visible even when the part as a whole passes.

## Success criteria from the manuscript (Chapter III, Experiments 1–3)

| Part | Experiment | Criterion | Pre-development survey benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part II | 1 — usefulness and relevance | **≥ 70% positive** | 84% agreed FinSight would help them manage records |
| Part III | 2 — clarity and decision support | **Majority positive (> 50%)** | 79% agreed the overviews and insights would help |
| Part IV | 3 — usability and record management | **Majority positive (> 50%)** | 86% agreed it would make record handling easier |

The benchmark column is what respondents said *before* the system existed. Post-use scores that fall well below those figures mean the built system did not deliver what people expected of the idea — that gap is worth reporting explicitly, not just the pass/fail.

## Items to watch regardless of whether the criteria pass

These map to known limitations already measured in testing. Report them individually even if the overall percentages pass.

| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **19 — trust in the numbers** | The whole system's value depends on this. A pass on clarity with low trust is a failure in practice. |
| **23 — understanding the three setup figures** | Available funds, expected monthly expenses and operating days drive every insight. If owners misunderstand them, every downstream number is wrong regardless of how well the software works. |
| **28 — receipt read correctly** | OCR measured 95% date / 95% vendor / 100% amount on a 20-image corpus that was mostly clean printed receipts. Real creased thermal paper performs worse. This item is the real-world check. |
| **29 — noticed values were editable** | The design assumes owners review before saving. If they do not notice, OCR errors become silent bad data. |
| **33 — false alarms** | Unusual-expense detection is known to over-flag categories with only about five records. Every reported false alarm is evidence for retuning it. |
| **Task 8 in the script — "did they think it was telling them whether to buy?"** | FinSight deliberately does not give a verdict. Owners reading it as advice is a safety issue, not a usability one. |

## Sample size

The manuscript does not state a target number of UAT participants. Set one with the adviser before starting. As a rule of thumb, 5–8 participants per segment surfaces most usability problems; percentage-based success criteria are shaky below about 10 respondents overall, so report the raw counts alongside any percentage.

## Segments to recruit

The three experiments target three different people, who may or may not be the same person in a small business:

1. **Owners** — decision makers (Part II is primarily theirs)
2. **Operators** — those who read and act on the figures (Part III)
3. **Whoever handles the records day to day** — often a family member or staff (Part IV)

Record which role each participant holds. If one person fills all three roles, note that too — it is a finding about how these businesses actually run.
